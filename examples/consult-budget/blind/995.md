$$R_m \;=\; \mathrm{GF}(2)[x^{\pm1},y^{\pm1}]\,/\,(x^m-1,\;y^m-1).$$

## Why (C) is exactly the right condition

Let $m$ be odd, $\zeta$ a primitive $m$‑th root of unity in $\overline{\mathrm{GF}(2)}$.

1. **$R_m$ is a product of finite fields.** Since $\gcd(m,2)=1$, $x^m-1$ is squarefree, so
$$R_m\;\cong\;\prod_{\text{orbits }O\text{ of }\langle 2\rangle\text{ on }(\mathbb Z/m)^2} \mathrm{GF}(2^{d_O}),$$
where $(j,k)\mapsto(\zeta^j,\zeta^k)$ and $d_O=|O|$. The image of $f$ in the factor for the orbit of $(j,k)$ is exactly the value $f(\zeta^j,\zeta^k)$.

2. **Cubes factorwise.** An element of a product of fields is a cube iff it is a cube in each factor. In $\mathrm{GF}(2^d)^*$ the cubes have index $\gcd(3,2^d-1)$: index $3$ when $d$ is even, index $1$ when $d$ is odd. Hence
$$f\text{ is a cube in }R_m \iff f(\zeta^j,\zeta^k)\text{ is a cube in }\mathrm{GF}(2)(\zeta^j,\zeta^k)^*\ \text{for every }(j,k)$$
(with $0$ always counting). **So (C) is not merely sufficient — it is equivalent to "every nonzero value is a cube."**

3. **Descent to $F_{2^{18}}$ at $m=37$.** Here $2$ has order $36$ and $2^{18}\equiv-1\pmod{37}$. For $f$ symmetric under $(x,y)\mapsto(x^{-1},y^{-1})$,
$$f(\zeta^j,\zeta^k)^{2^{18}}=f(\zeta^{-j},\zeta^{-k})=f(\zeta^j,\zeta^k),$$
so **all values lie in $F_{2^{18}}$** (inversion *is* $\mathrm{Frob}^{18}$). And for $w\in F_{2^{18}}^*$, $w$ is a cube in $F_{2^{36}}^*$ iff it is a cube in $F_{2^{18}}^*$: indeed $(2^{36}-1)/3=\tfrac{2^{18}-1}{3}(2^{18}+1)$ with $2^{18}+1\equiv2\pmod3$, so $w^{(2^{36}-1)/3}=\big(w^{(2^{18}-1)/3}\big)^{2}=1\iff w^{(2^{18}-1)/3}=1$. **Therefore (C) in $R_{37}$ gives cubes in $F_{2^{18}}^*$, i.e. period dividing $(2^{18}-1)/3=87381$.**
