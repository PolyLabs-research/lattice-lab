# Half-Frobenius confinement: why the six rules have only cube eigenvalues at m = 37

## 1. Answer in one paragraph

At m = 37 the Frobenius that generates Gal(F(2^18)/F(2^9)), x ↦ x^(2^9), acts on characters as
multiplication by 2^9 ≡ −6 (mod 37), and 6² ≡ −1 (mod 37). So any polynomial on the 37-torus whose
support is invariant under the dilation (x, y) ↦ (x^6, y^6) has all its eigenvalues in F(2^9), and
since 9 is odd, 3 ∤ 2^9 − 1, so **every element of F(2^9) is a cube in F(2^18)**. Each of the six
rules becomes such a polynomial once it is multiplied by a few rule-90 factors t_w = x^w + x^−w,
whose eigenvalues ζ^j + ζ^−j are cubes at 37 (rule 90's own confinement there). For example, with
a = x + 1/x, b = y + 1/y and W = A4 + K8 + B4 + self,

    W(x², y²) · t(x) t(y) t(xy) t(x/y)  =  Σ over the 8 points (±7, ±5), (±5, ±7)   (over GF(2), exactly)

and 6·(7, 5) = (42, 30) ≡ (5, −7) (mod 37) is again in that shell. Three more of the six are W times
rule-90 products (K8+M8+E4 = ab·W, C4+L8+M8 = (a+b)·W, A4+B4+C4+L8+E4+self = (a+1)(b+1)·W); C4+L8+M8+E4
and the triangle rule have identities of the same kind (§4). Rewritten with p = t_{(j+k)/2},
q = t_{(j−k)/2} (rule-90 eigenvalues), the eigenvalue of W at (j, k) is

    λ_W = Tr_{F(2^18)/F(2^9)}(p^(2^9) q) / (p q (p + q)),

a trace (in F(2^9), so a cube) over a product of rule-90 values (cubes): a cube.

The general mechanism (§3), implemented as `condition(f, m)` in `mechanism.py`, is: *f(x^r, y^r)
equals a product of rule-90-type units times a core C, and C times a product N of rule-90 orbit
factors is, on the m-torus, invariant under the dilation by n = 2^g (mod m) for a proper divisor g
of s = sord(m)*. Then every eigenvalue of f lies in the group generated by F(2^g)* and the rule-90
eigenvalues t_j, which confines it whenever both lie in a proper subgroup of F(2^s)* of the same
index. The special case N = 1 with no units, "f is invariant under a dilation by a power of 2
modulo m", puts every eigenvalue in the subfield F(2^g) and needs no rule-90 input at all; it
accounts for most of the census's small-field "coincidences" (§6).

## 2. Setting and notation

m is odd, ζ a primitive m-th root of unity in F(2^d), d = ord_m(2), and s = sord(m) is the least j
with 2^j ≡ ±1 (mod m). A rule f is a Laurent polynomial over GF(2) with f(1/x, 1/y) = f (all atlas
rules; for the triangle, f is the plane rule s). Its eigenvalue at a character c = (j, k) ∈ (Z/m)²
is λ_f(c) = f(ζ^j, ζ^k). Three elementary facts are used throughout:

* (F1) λ_{gh} = λ_g λ_h, λ_{g+h} = λ_g + λ_h, and λ depends on g only modulo (x^m − 1, y^m − 1).
* (F2) Frobenius: λ_g(c)² = λ_g(2c) for g with GF(2) coefficients; more generally
  λ_g(c)^(2^i) = λ_g(2^i c), and λ_{g(x^r, y^r)}(c) = λ_g(rc).
* (F3) Symmetric f: λ_f(c)^(2^s) = λ_f(±c) = λ_f(c), so λ_f(c) ∈ F(2^s).

For a vector w ∈ Z², t_w = x^w + x^−w is rule 90 along w; λ_{t_w}(c) = t_{w·c} where
t_j = ζ^j + ζ^−j. Let **T_m** be the subgroup of F(2^s)* generated by the nonzero t_j, j ∈ Z/m. It is
finite and closed under squaring (t_j² = t_{2j}), so squaring permutes it.

**Dickson and Ψ polynomials.** D_e(t) is defined by x^e + x^−e = D_e(x + 1/x) (over GF(2):
D_1 = t, D_{e+1} = t D_e + D_{e−1}). For odd e ≥ 3 define Ψ_e by Ψ_e(x + 1/x) = x^(−φ(e)/2) Φ_e(x)
(Φ_e the cyclotomic polynomial; it is palindromic of even degree, so Ψ_e is a polynomial over GF(2)).
Since x^e + x^−e = x^−e (1 + x^e)² = x^−e ∏_{e'|e} Φ_{e'}(x)² over GF(2),

    D_e(t) = t · ∏_{1<e'|e} Ψ_{e'}(t)²                                                    (1)

(`mechanism._psi` computes Ψ_e from (1); e.g. Ψ_3 = t + 1, Ψ_5 = t² + t + 1, Ψ_7 = t³ + t² + 1, Ψ_9 = t³ + t + 1).

## 3. The mechanism and its proof

**Lemma 1 (dilation-invariant polynomials have values in a subfield).** If g | d and B is a
Laurent polynomial over GF(2) with B(x^n, y^n) ≡ B(x, y) (mod x^m − 1, y^m − 1), n ≡ 2^g (mod m),
then λ_B(c) ∈ F(2^g) for every c.

*Proof.* By (F1) and (F2), λ_B(c)^(2^g) = λ_B(2^g c) = λ_{B(x^n, y^n)}(c) = λ_B(c). ∎

**Lemma 2 (units).** Let c be a character and w a vector.
(a) λ_{t_w}(c) = t_{w·c} lies in T_m ∪ {0}, and is 0 only if w·c ≡ 0 (mod m).
(b) For odd e ≥ 3 with gcd(e, m) = 1, Ψ_e(t_j) ∈ T_m for j ≢ 0, and Ψ_e(t_0) = Ψ_e(0) ∈ {0, 1}.

*Proof.* (a) t_j = ζ^−j (ζ^{2j} + 1) vanishes iff ζ^{2j} = 1 iff m | j (m odd). (b) For j ≢ 0 and
every e' | e, e'j ≢ 0 (mod m) because gcd(e', m) = 1, so D_{e'}(t_j) = t_{e'j} ≠ 0 and t_j ≠ 0. By
Möbius inversion of (1), Ψ_e(t_j)² = ∏_{e'|e} (t_{e'j}/t_j)^{μ(e/e')} ∈ T_m, and since squaring
permutes T_m, Ψ_e(t_j) ∈ T_m. ∎

For v primitive and G the rule's symmetry group (matrices with entries in {−1, 0, 1} fixing f), let
O_v = ∏ t_w and P_{e,v} = ∏ Ψ_e(t_w), the products over one w from each pair ±w in the orbit Gv. By
Lemma 2, every nonzero eigenvalue of a product of O_v's and P_{e,v}'s (gcd(e, m) = 1) lies in T_m.

**Lemma 3 (lines).** For w primitive, {c : w·c ≡ 0 (mod m)} = {k c_0 : k ∈ Z/m} with
c_0 = (w_2, −w_1). If C is symmetric, C(z^{w_2}, z^{−w_1}) = H(z + 1/z) for a polynomial H over
GF(2), and λ_C(k c_0) = H(t_k). If H = 0 or H = t^i ∏ Ψ_e^{k_e} with gcd(e, m) = 1, then every
nonzero λ_C(k c_0) lies in T_m.

*Proof.* A primitive w is the first row of a matrix in SL_2(Z), which identifies the kernel of
c ↦ w·c with a cyclic group generated by c_0. The restriction is a symmetric one-variable Laurent
polynomial, hence a polynomial in z + 1/z (as z^e + z^−e = D_e). The last claim is Lemma 2. ∎

**Theorem (half-Frobenius confinement).** Let f be a symmetric rule, m odd, s = sord(m), r ∈ {1, 2}.
Suppose

1. f(x^r, y^r) = U · C exactly, with U a product of O_v's and P_{e,v}'s (gcd(e, m) = 1);
2. N is a product of O_v's (v primitive) and B := C · N is invariant modulo (x^m − 1, y^m − 1) under
   (x, y) ↦ (x^n, y^n), n = 2^g mod m, for some divisor g of s with g < s;
3. for every w in the orbits making up N, C restricted to the line {w·c ≡ 0} is as in Lemma 3.

Then every nonzero eigenvalue of f lies in the subgroup ⟨F(2^g)*, T_m⟩ of F(2^s)*. If U = N = 1,
every eigenvalue lies in F(2^g).

*Proof.* Let c be a character. By (F2), λ_f(rc) = λ_U(c) λ_C(c), and as r is invertible mod m every
character is of the form rc. If λ_N(c) ≠ 0, then λ_C(c) = λ_B(c)/λ_N(c) with λ_B(c) ∈ F(2^g)
(Lemma 1) and λ_N(c) ∈ T_m (Lemma 2a). If λ_N(c) = 0, then w·c ≡ 0 for some w in N (Lemma 2a), and
λ_C(c) ∈ T_m ∪ {0} by condition 3 and Lemma 3. In both cases, and with λ_U(c) ∈ T_m ∪ {0}
(Lemma 2), λ_f(rc) ∈ ⟨F(2^g)*, T_m⟩ ∪ {0}. ∎

**Corollary (the returned q).** In a cyclic group, the q-th powers form the unique subgroup of index
q. Let p^k (p prime) divide (2^s − 1)/(2^g − 1) and suppose every nonzero t_j is a p^k-th power in
F(2^s)*. Then F(2^g)* (order 2^g − 1, which divides (2^s − 1)/p^k) and T_m both lie in the p^k-th
powers, so every nonzero eigenvalue of f is a p^k-th power in F(2^s)*. Multiplying over the primes
p and by (2^d − 1)/(2^s − 1) (eigenvalues lie in F(2^s), F3), every nonzero eigenvalue of f is a
q-th power in F(2^d)*, with

    q = (2^d − 1)/(2^s − 1) · ∏_p p^{k_p}          (mixed case),
    q = (2^d − 1)/(2^s − 1) · (2^s − 1)/(2^g − 1) = (2^d − 1)/(2^g − 1)     (pure case U = N = 1).

`condition(f, m)` searches U (units O_v, P_{e,v} with |v| ≤ 3, e ≤ 15, stripped from f(x^r, y^r) by
exact division), N (up to three O_v with |v| ≤ 2), r ∈ {1, 2} and all proper divisors g of s, checks
conditions 1–3 literally, and returns the largest q obtained, or 1. The p^k are found by testing the
rule-90 values t_j (one per Frobenius orbit) against each odd prime p < 20000 dividing 2^s − 1: this
is rule 90's own confinement, the same input mechanism 1 (splitting) relies on, and the only field
computation in `condition`; no eigenvalue of f is computed. It is returned only for a proper
confinement: in the mixed case, if some p^{k_p} > 1; the pure case always is. If f(x^r, y^r) is
itself a product of units (C = 1), the t_j alone confine it (splitting); `condition` reports that too.

## 4. The six rules at m = 37

At 37: d = 36, s = 18, 2^9 ≡ −6 and 6² ≡ −1 (mod 37); so n = 2^9 acts like ±6. Rule 90 is confined:
every t_j is a cube in F(2^18) (its odd part is 87381 = (2^18 − 1)/3; card). With g = 9, the
prime power 3 divides (2^18 − 1)/(2^9 − 1) = 513 = 27·19, so q_s = 3 and
q = 3 · (2^36 − 1)/(2^18 − 1) = 3 · 262145 = 786435: *every eigenvalue is a cube of F(2^18)*,
i.e. has order dividing 87381*. (In F(2^36) every element of F(2^18) is trivially a cube, so the
honest statement of "cube in F(2^18)" in the card's F(2^d) convention is "786435-th power".)

The exact identities over GF(2) (checked symbolically by `proposal.py`), with a = t(x), b = t(y),
t(z) = z + 1/z, O_{10} = t(x)t(y), O_{11} = t(xy)t(x/y), and Sh(u, v) the sum over the symmetric
images of (u, v):

| Rule | identity | B invariant under ×6 mod 37 because |
| --- | --- | --- |
| W = A4+K8+B4+self | W(x², y²)·O_{10}·O_{11} = Sh(7,5) | 6·(7,5) ≡ (5,−7) |
| K8+M8+E4 | = a b · W (units O_{10}) | as W |
| C4+L8+M8 | = (a+b) · W; (a+b)(x², y²) = O_{11} | as W |
| A4+B4+C4+L8+E4+self | = (a+1)(b+1) · W; (a+1)(b+1)(x²,y²) = P_{3,(1,0)}² | as W |
| V = C4+L8+M8+E4 | V(x², y²)·O_{10} = Sh(7,7) + Sh(5,5) | 6·(7,7) ≡ (5,5) |
| triangle VS+T9+T13+self (its s = S) | S(x², y²)·O_{(1,0)}·O_{(2,1)} = Hex(14,12) + Hex(12,10) | 6·(7,6) ≡ (5,−1) |

For the triangle, coordinates are those of `data/rules.json` (hexagonal symmetry
(p, q) ↦ (p − q, p) and (p, q) ↦ (q, p)); O_{(1,0)} = t(x)t(y)t(xy), O_{(2,1)} = t(x²y)t(xy²)t(x/y),
and Hex(u, v) is the 12-point hexagonal orbit; the two orbits have hexagonal norms
p² − pq + q² = 43·4 and 31·4, and 43 ≡ −31 (mod 37) (norms scale by 6² ≡ −1). Likewise the square
shells: 7² + 5² = 74 ≡ 0, 7²+7² = 98 ≡ 24 ≡ −13 ≡ −(5²+5²) (mod 37).

The lines of condition 3 are the axes and diagonals (square) and the six lattice directions
(triangle); on them the cores restrict to (t+1)^4, (t+1)^8, t²(t+1)^4 and (t+1)^4 (t³+t+1)^4 =
Ψ_3^4 Ψ_9^4, all products of Ψ's with gcd(e, 37) = 1. So the theorem applies to all six.

**Closed forms.** Put p = t_{(j+k)/2}, q = t_{(j−k)/2} (rule-90 values; 1/2 = 19 mod 37). Then
p + q = t_{j/2} t_{k/2} and pq = t_j + t_k, and every square D4 rule is a symmetric polynomial in
p and q. The "ring" R_k (boundary of the square of radius k: R_1 = N4+D4, R_2 = A4+K8+B4,
R_3 = C4+L8+M8+E4) has eigenvalue (p D_k(p)² + q D_k(q)²)/(p + q) at every m. So
W = R_2 + R_0 = (D_6(p)/p + D_6(q)/q)/(p + q) and V = R_3 = (p D_6(p) + q D_6(q))/(p + q). At 37,
D_6(t_x) = t_{6x} = t_{−6x} = t_x^(2^9) = F^9(t_x), so

    λ_W = (q p^(2^9) + p q^(2^9)) / (pq(p + q)) = Tr_{18/9}(p^(2^9) q) / (pq(p + q)),
    λ_V = (N_{18/9}(p) + N_{18/9}(q)) / (p + q).

Traces and norms to F(2^9) are cubes, p, q, p + q are cubes: hence the cubes. This is how the
mechanism was found (§7).

**Lean.** `proofs/LatticeProofs/Pilot.lean` proves, for each of the six rules D,
`period 37 D ∣ Nat.lcm 511 (period 37 r90Offsets)` (`period_w_dvd`, `period_k8m8e4_dvd`,
`period_c4l8m8_dvd`, `period_a4b4c4l8e4self_dvd`, `period_v_dvd`, `period_tri_dvd`), axioms
propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound only. It also proves rule 90's confinement,
`period_r90_dvd : period 37 r90Offsets ∣ 87381`: Φ_37 is irreducible over GF(2), so t = z + 1/z
generates F(2^18) = GF(2)[t]/Ψ_37, and v(t) = t^15 + t^13 + t^12 + t^11 + t^9 + t^7 + t^6 + t
satisfies v(t)³ ≡ t (mod Ψ_37) (certified polynomial identity); v(t) is fixed by the 2^18-th power
because t is, so t^87381 = v(t)^(2^18 − 1) = 1. Hence, unconditionally, `period 37 D ∣ 87381` for
all six (`period_w_dvd_87381`, …, `period_tri_dvd_87381`). The proofs follow §3 literally: the GF(2)
identity, the half-Frobenius permutation of the shells, rule-90 orders on the t-factors, and the
lines handled by (t+1)² = t(z³)/t(z) and t(z⁹)/t(z).

**Why rule 90 is confined at 37** (an arithmetic explanation; the Lean proof above certifies it directly). A t ∈ F(2^18)
is a cube iff N_{F(2^18)/F_4}(t) = 1. For t = t_1 = N_{F(2^36)/F(2^18)}(1 + ζ) this norm is
∏_{h a square mod 37}(1 + ζ^h) (checked = 1 by computation), the reduction modulo the inert prime 2
of ±∏_{h QR}(1 − ζ_37^h) = ±√37 ε^{−1} with ε = 6 + √37 the fundamental unit of Q(√37) (class
number 1); √37 ≡ 1 and ε ≡ 1 modulo 2, so the norm is 1.

## 5. What makes 37 special for these rules

Three things are needed. (i) s even with an odd-index subfield inside the cubes: at 37, s = 18 and
F(2^9) (3 ∤ 511). (ii) Rule 90 confined (t_j cubes), an arithmetic fact about 37 (§4). (iii) The
multiplied-out rule must be invariant under ×2^{s/2}. Whenever 2^s ≡ −1 (mod m), n = 2^{s/2} is a
square root of −1 modulo m, and on the square lattice ×n maps a vector (a, b) to (na, nb); it acts
on the shells (D4-orbits) by pairing a shell of norm a² + b² with one of norm ≡ −(a² + b²). Small
shells are paired only when n is small: at 37, n = ±6 and 37 = 6² + 1, so 37 = (6 + i)(6 − i) has a
Gaussian prime factor of length √37 and the shells (7, 5) (norm 74 = 2·37; 5 + 7i = (1 + i)(6 + i)), (7, 7) ↔ (5, 5) (norms 98 ≡ −50) and, on the hexagonal lattice (37 = 7² − 7·3 + 3²),
(7, 6) ↔ (6, 5) (norms 43 ≡ −31) are paired. At 101 (n = 10), 197 (n = 14) or 269 (n = 82), where
rule 90 is also confined, the pairing involves vectors of length about n, out of reach of rules with
translations of length ≤ 3 (whose multiplied-out forms have length ≤ 2·3 + 3); the search finds
no instance there. At 95 and 111
(composite; s = d = 36, so no power of 2 is ≡ −1 and no dilation 2^g is a square root of −1) the
search finds no 2^g, g | 36 of the right index, that pairs the shells of the six identities. That is why 37
is the six rules' only shortfall, and why only these rules (those with an identity of this kind) are confined there.

## 6. Census coverage and predictions

`condition` was run on all 1,274 rules at every odd m from 9 to 301 (147 sizes), and every claim
was checked against the eigenvalues computed directly in F(2^d) (`scratch/sweep.py`, results in
`scratch/swc*.jsonl`): **838 claims, none false**. By kind:

* **mixed (the new half-Frobenius mechanism with rule-90 factors): 6 claims, exactly the six rules
  at m = 37** and nowhere else up to 301. Rule 90 itself is confined (some t_j-power test succeeds)
  only at m = 37, 95, 101, 111, 141, 197, 199 (7th powers), 203 (3rd, 5th, 7th powers) and 269 in
  this range; at the others the identities of §4 have no analogue among the atlas's small shells.
* **pure (f invariant under a dilation by 2^g mod m): 445 claims.** At multiples of 3 these are the
  nine rules h(x³, y³) (the known dilation mechanism, here with n = 2^g ≡ ±1 mod m/3), plus the
  trivial rule self. At **m = 13** they are 21 rules, invariant under ×8 (8 = 2³, 8² ≡ −1 mod 13;
  e.g. 8·(2, 3) ≡ (3, −2)) or ×4 (2², for the hexagonal ones), whose eigenvalues therefore lie in
  F(2³) or F(2²), both inside the cubes of F(2^6).
* **splitting (f(x^r, y^r) a product of units): 387 claims**, at the m where rule 90 is confined.

**Census entries explained: 24 of 69**: the six at 37 and 18 of the small-field coincidences at 13
(M8, M8+self, A4+C4, A4+C4+self, B4+E4, B4+E4+self, A4+C4+M8, A4+C4+M8+self, B4+M8+E4+self,
A4+B4+C4+E4, A4+B4+C4+E4+self, A4+B4+C4+M8+E4; triangle T13, T13+self; hexagon H13, H13+self,
H3+H5, H3+H5+self). **Not explained: 45**: 34 others at 13, and all at 21 (6), 25 (3), 29
(K8+M8+self), 69 (triangle E+VO+T7+T9+self) and 115 (N4+D4+B4+C4). These are not dilation invariant
and, where rule 90 is not confined (13, 21, 25, 29, 69, 115), the mixed form cannot apply.

**Predictions beyond the census.** Up to m = 301 the mechanism predicts no new confinement of
the mixed kind: the six rules at 37 are its only instances among the atlas's rules. Its pure
kind predicts the subfield confinements listed above at every size (all verified), and the
mixed kind would apply to larger rules: any rule whose multiplied-out form pairs shells under
×2^{s/2} at an m where rule 90 is confined, e.g. at 101 (2^25 ≡ 10, 10² ≡ −1) shells paired
by ×10 such as (10, 1) ↔ (−1, 10), which need translations of length about 10, beyond the atlas.

## 7. How it was found

1. Factoring the six polynomials over GF(2) (Kronecker substitution plus recombination) showed
   that four of the five square rules are W = A4+K8+B4+self times ab, a+b or (a+1)(b+1), all
   rule-90 products, and that W is the boundary of the 5×5 square plus its centre, V the boundary of
   the 7×7 square.
2. In the coordinates p = t_{(j+k)/2}, q = t_{(j−k)/2}, these "ring" rules become
   (φ(p) + φ(q))/(p + q) with φ a combination of Dickson polynomials; at 37 every D_e is a power of
   Frobenius on rule-90 values (2 is a primitive root mod 37), and D_6 = F^9, which turned the
   numerators into the trace and the norm from F(2^18) to F(2^9).
3. Translating back to lattice polynomials gave the identities of §4, and the dilation-invariance
   form generalised to the triangle, found by a small search over products of hexagonal orbit
   factors O_v.

## 8. What the condition does not do

The theorem is a sufficient condition and is proved in full (§3); the only external input is
rule 90's own confinement, which `condition` reads off the rule-90 values t_j (one per Frobenius
orbit), as the splitting mechanism does. It does not explain *why* rule 90 is confined at a given
m in general; at 37 it is proved in Lean (§4).


It is sufficient, not necessary: it searches a finite family (|v| ≤ 2 for N, three factors, units
up to |v| ≤ 3 and Ψ_e with e ≤ 15, r ∈ {1, 2}, primes < 20000 for the rule-90 confinement). It does
not include mechanism 2 (powers f = g^k): five all-cube rules at 37 that are cubes g³ are not
claimed. It does not claim the small-field coincidences that are not dilation invariant (§6).
