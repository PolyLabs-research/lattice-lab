**Per-pass records for BB_756_16** are attached as `BB_756_16_passes.csv`, one row per (pass, part):

- `bound`: pass b asks whether there is a Z-logical of weight ≤ b (bounds 2 to 29, in order);
- `found`: whether that pass found one (false for every pass);
- `part`: which of the pass's 16 parts (the tree is split by a hash of the node's partial support at depth 6, and each node is searched by exactly one part);
- `nodes`, `seconds`: nodes visited and wall time for that part.

Settings: kind Z, anchors 0 and lm, packing cut on, 16 parts on 4 cores. In total that is about 2.23 × 10^11 nodes and 7 hours of wall time. The bound-29 pass alone was 1.41 × 10^11 nodes and 4.5 hours. From bound 17 on, the node count grows by a factor of 2.6 to 2.75 per unit of bound.

**Since then, your cluster engine has done most of our exact work.** We are computing exact distances for every weight-6 BB code (A and B each three monomials) on every torus up to n = 400, and `-engine=cc` at commit 8fe913e has been our exact engine since late October. Some numbers, in case they are useful:

- **A first comparison.** We ran both engines on 20 codes with n up to 300 and d from 16 to 24. The cluster engine returned d for all 20, the slowest in 267 s. The MaxSAT engine finished 10 within 2 hours; the other 10 (k = 4, d 22 to 24) stopped at lb 18.
- **Against our own search.** On 16 tori up to n = 300, the two agree on all 528 classes where both finished. The cluster engine also settled 154 classes that our search had left at 23 ≤ d ≤ 24. All of them have d = 24, with a median of 155 s and a maximum of 410 s.
- **At scale.** On tori with n = 302 to 396 it has returned about 12,100 exact distances so far, with no errors. For every one, our own search found a Z-logical of exactly that weight, checked by our own GF(2) routine, and none of our searches found a lighter logical than the d it returned.
- **Speed.** On these codes the time per class grows by about 7× per +2 in d.
