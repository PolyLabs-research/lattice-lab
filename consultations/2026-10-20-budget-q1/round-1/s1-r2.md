## Summary of what I found and checked

**Setup, and why the values lie in F_{2^18}.** With ζ of order 37 and 2 of order 36 mod 37, Frobenius `φ: v↦v²` acts on ζ by `ζ↦ζ²`, so `φ^18` acts by `ζ↦ζ^{2^18}=ζ^{-1}` (since `2^18≡-1 mod 37`). Each `f` is invariant under `(x,y)↦(1/x,1/y)`, hence `φ^18(f(ζ^j,ζ^k))=f(ζ^{-j},ζ^{-k})=f(ζ^j,ζ^k)`, so every value is fixed by `φ^18` and lies in `F_{2^18}`. (Verified: all 1369 points, all five — `inF218 = True`.)

**The cube criterion.** `F_4 ⊂ F_{2^18}` because `2 | 18`, and `|F_4*|=3`. For `v∈F_{2^18}*`,
> `v` is a cube ⟺ `v^{(2^18−1)/3}=1` ⟺ `N_{F_{2^18}/F_4}(v)=1`, where `(2^18−1)/3 = 87381`.

So "every value is a cube" is exactly "period divides 87381," as the problem states.

**The general sufficient condition I propose and proved.**

> **Condition (C).** Let `R_m = GF(2)[x,y]/(x^m−1, y^m−1)`. If `f ≡ g³ (mod x^m−1, y^m−1)` for some `g∈GF(2)[x,y]` — i.e. **`f` is a cube in the group ring `R_m`** — then for *every* root‑of‑unity point `P=(ζ^j,ζ^k)` the value `f(P)=g(P)³` is a cube in `F_{2^{36}}*`, hence (being in `F_{2^18}`) a cube in `F_{2^18}*`. The same statement with `g^q` forces every value to be a `q`‑th power.

The proof that (C) suffices is one line: `f(P)=g(P)³`. The nontrivial content is that (C) *holds* for the five rules and for `t`.

**Proof that (C) holds at m=37 (machine‑checked).** I verified in two equivalent ways:

1. *Pointwise.* For every `(j,k)∈(Z/37)²` and each `f∈{f1,…,f5}` and for `t(x)=x+1/x`, the value is a cube even in the full field `F_{2^{36}}`: `f(P)^{(2^36−1)/3}=1`. This is strictly stronger than the F_{2^18} claim (`allcube = True`, and `cube-in-F2^36 everywhere = True` at all 1369 points).
2. *As a ring identity.* Because `f(P)` is a cube at **every** component of `R_37`, `f` is a cube in `R_37`. I constructed an explicit `g_i∈GF(2)[x,y]` for each `i` (via Frobenius‑equivariant cube‑root selection on each orbit followed by inverse transform) and checked `g_i*g_i*g_i ≡ f_i` at all 37² points — passed for all five, and likewise a univariate `g_t` with `g_t³ ≡ x+1/x`.

So the reason is clean and correct: **these rules are perfect cubes in the group ring mod `(x^37−1, y^37−1)`.**

## Where I think the project is hoping for something that isn't there

The natural hope is that the cube is explained by a *clean rule‑level* reason: a small/symmetric cube root `g`, a dilation symmetry, or a subfield. I checked and these all **fail**:

- **No subfield.** The values do *not* lie in any proper subfield of `F_{2^18}`: the smallest `d` with every value in `F_{2^d}` is `d=18` for all five. So "cube" is a genuine index‑3 condition, not "lands in `F_{2^6}` (order 63) which happens to be cubes."
- **No cube‑root dilation symmetry.** `f` is **not** invariant under dilation by the order‑3 elements `c∈{10,26}` of `(Z/37)*` (`10=2^24`, `26=2^12`). So the "relative norm `f=g·g^{(10)}·g^{(26)}`" story is false (consistent with the no‑subfield fact).
- **No low‑weight / symmetric cube root.** Searching all sums of orbits `O(a,b)` with `a,b≤6` up to 4 orbits found no `g` with `g³=f`, and no `h` with `f=h·h^{[4]}·h^{[16]}` (the Frobenius‑triple form, exponent `1+4+16=21=3·7`). The cube roots my construction produces are **dense** (≈600–725 terms for the `f_i`; even `t=x+1/x` needs an 18‑of‑37 support cube root).

My reading: the phenomenon is real and has a crisp *characterization* (cube in `R_m`), but it does **not** come from a simple symmetric sub‑rule. If the project’s write‑up suggests a tidy structural generator `g`, that part is likely to be wrong; the honest invariant is the ring‑cube property plus the `N_{/F_4}=1` criterion.
