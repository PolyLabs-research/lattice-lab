## 1. The exact reason, stated as a theorem (proved)

Let R = GF(2)[x,y]/(x³⁷−1, y³⁷−1). Since 2 generates (Z/37)*, the Fourier transform gives R ≅ GF(2) × F₂³⁶³⁸ (one factor per line through 0 in (Z/37)²), Frobenius f ↦ f² acts on R as the dilation f(x,y) ↦ f(x²,y²), and the 18 dilations by 4ⁱ mod 37 are exactly the dilations by the quadratic residues QR₃₇ = {1,3,4,7,9,10,11,12,16,21,25,26,27,28,30,33,34,36}.

**Theorem A (proved).** For any f ∈ R the following are equivalent:
(i) every nonzero value f(ζʲ,ζᵏ) is a cube in F₂³⁶*;
(ii) f^{(2³⁶−1)/3 + 1} = f in R;
(iii) f(x,y)·∏_{d∈QR₃₇} f(xᵈ,yᵈ) ≡ f(x,y) mod (x³⁷−1, y³⁷−1).
If moreover f is symmetric under (x,y)→(1/x,1/y) (so its values lie in F₂¹⁸), these are equivalent to
(iv) every nonzero value is a cube in F₂¹⁸*, i.e. lies in the subgroup of order 87381.

*Proof.* u ∈ F₂³⁶* is a cube iff u^{(2³⁶−1)/3} = 1; this holds componentwise in R, and 0 is fine, giving (i)⇔(ii). (2³⁶−1)/3 = Σ_{i=0}^{17} 4ⁱ and f^{4ⁱ} = f(x^{4ⁱ},y^{4ⁱ}), giving (ii)⇔(iii). For (iv): if u ∈ F₂¹⁸ and u = v³ with v ∈ F₂³⁶, then v^{2¹⁸−1} is a cube root of unity ω with ω^{2¹⁸+1} = v^{2³⁶−1} = 1; since 2¹⁸+1 ≡ 2 mod 3, ω = 1 and v ∈ F₂¹⁸. ∎

**General form (proved, same argument).** For an odd prime m with n = ord_m(2) and q | 2ⁿ−1: all nonzero values of f are q‑th powers in F₂ⁿ ⟺ f^{(2ⁿ−1)/q+1} ≡ f mod (xᵐ−1, yᵐ−1). For q = 3, n even, this reads f·∏_{i<n/2} f(x^{4ⁱ},y^{4ⁱ}) ≡ f. For symmetric f, "cube in F₂ⁿ" = "cube in F₂^{n/2}" iff n/2 is even (if n/2 is odd, 3 ∤ 2^{n/2}−1 and the statement is vacuous — worth noting for the project's period claims at other moduli). The congruence (iii) is itself the certificate: I verified it by machine for f1–f5 and for t at m = 37 (and that it fails for all five at 101 and 197, see §3).

So the period of each of the five automata on the 37×37 torus divides 87381 — this part is now a checked polynomial identity, machine‑provable in R.

## 2. Is there a structural cause? What I could and could not establish

The cube condition is a product of 11 independent conditions (the cubic character, with values in F₄* ≅ Z/3, is constant on lines, and D₄ acting on P¹(F₃₇) has 11 orbits: {0,∞}, {±1}, {±6}, and 8 orbits of size 4). Each is "Res(Q, f(z,zˢ)) = 1 in F₄", where Q = ∏_{d∈QR}(z+ζᵈ) ∈ F₄[z] is the quadratic‑residue half of Φ₃₇.

**For t the cause is number‑theoretic (proved, checked).** t(x) = x³⁶(1+x)² in R, and x³⁶ = (x¹²)³, so t is a cube iff 1+ζ is a cube in F₂³⁶, i.e. iff N_{F₂³⁶/F₄}(1+ζ) = ∏_{r∈QR}(1+ζʳ) = 1. This is the reduction mod a prime above 2 of ∏_{r∈QR}(1−ζʳ) ∈ Z[ζ], which by Dirichlet's class number formula equals ±√p·ε^{−h} (ε, h = fundamental unit and class number of Q(√p); p ≡ 1 mod 4). In F₄ = Z[ω]/(2), ω = (1+√p)/2, we have √p = 2ω−1 ≡ 1, hence:

> **Theorem B.** For a prime p ≡ 5 mod 8 with 2 a primitive root, t(ζ_p) is a cube in F₂^{p−1} (equivalently in F₂^{(p−1)/2} when (p−1)/2 is even) iff εʰ ≡ 1 mod 2 in F₄.

For p = 37: ε = 6+√37 = 5+2ω ≡ 1, h = 1. ✓. Checked by machine for every such prime p < 400: cube exactly for 37, 101, 197, 269, 349, 373, 389 (ε ≡ 1), not for 5, 13, 29, 53, 61, 149, 173, 181, 293, 317 (ε ≡ ω, ω²). The same mechanism makes every 1 + xᵃyᵇ, every monomial, every Φ_k(xᵃyᵇ), and hence every single orbit O(a,b) (e.g. O(2,0) = x³⁵y³⁵(x+y)²(1+xy)², O(2,2) = t(x²)t(y²)) a cube at 37.

**For f1–f5 I found no structural cause, and I believe there is none beyond Theorem A.** Checked: (a) none of the five factors into binomials/monomials in the Laurent ring (f2, f3 have factors (1+x)²(1+y)², (1+xy)(1+x/y) but the cofactors are generic; f1, f4, f5 have none); (b) the 11 restrictions f(z,zˢ) mod z³⁷−1 factor over GF(2) into generic irreducibles, not cyclotomic pieces; (c) decisively, at p = 101 and 197 — where 1+ζ *is* a cube — none of f1–f5 is a cube. So the five are not "cubes because of 37's unit"; their 11 cubic characters simply all vanish.

## 3. Where I think the project is wrong

The null model "once in 20,000" is wrong for this family. Enumerating all 1023 nonempty unions of the 10 orbits O(a,b), 3 ≥ a ≥ b ≥ 0:

| m | 13 | 29 | 37 | 41 | 53 | 61 | 101 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cubes / 1023 | 43 | 11 | **39** | 8 | 10 | 14 | 34 |

So ~1% are cubes at ordinary primes (zero lines, small supports, and multiplicative structure make the 11 characters far from independent), ~4% at 37 and 101 (Theorem B drives up the count via the single orbits and products such as (1+t(x²))(1+t(y²)) = 1+O(2,0)+O(2,2)). The five are five of 39 hits at 37 (the list of all 39 is reproducible from the identity in Theorem A); "none of the five does anything like it at 9…161" is selection, not a property of these polynomials. The project should (1) replace the heuristic with the exact criterion (iii), (2) report the full hit list, and (3) separate the t‑type hits, which have a cause, from the rest, which do not.

## 4. Registered predictions

1. (Theorem A) For any symmetric f at m = 37: period divides 87381 ⟺ f·∏_{d∈QR₃₇} f(xᵈ,yᵈ) ≡ f. Equivalently fails iff f·∏ ≠ f.
2. (Theorem B) For primes p ≡ 5 mod 8 with 2 a primitive root and p < 400 the cube status of t is exactly the list above; for larger such p it is "cube iff εʰ ≡ 1 mod 2". For p ≡ 3 mod 8 with 2 a primitive root, every symmetric f is a cube in F₂^{p−1} (trivially, since (p−1)/2 is odd).
3. f1–f5 are **not** cubes at m = 101 and m = 197 (computed), and I predict the family density of cubes at 269, 349, 373, 389 will be elevated (≈3–4%) versus ≈1% at 293, 317.
4. 1+x+x³ is a cube at 37 (computed) — univariate polynomials are cubes with probability ≈1/3, another reason the "20,000" figure is misleading.
