# Summary

Nine of the sixteen are not mysterious: each is **(a split rule) × (a cube)**, an exact identity in GF(2)[x^±1, y^±1]. The project missed this because the mechanisms were tested one at a time and no bivariate factorisation was ever done. The cofactor is one of three split rules — `t(x)t(y)`, `O(1,0)` (= rule 90 along both diagonals), or `(1+t(x))(1+t(y))` (= rule 150 along both axes) — so the 2-D question reduces to a 1-D one: *when are the eigenvalues of rule 90 / rule 150 cubes?* I prove a closed formula for the cubic character of those eigenvalues (it is a norm down to F₄), which (i) explains why rule 90 is confined at 37 (because 6+√37 is a unit of Z[√37] — this is the classical Martin–Odlyzko–Wolfram anomaly Π₃₇ = (2¹⁸−1)/3), (ii) explains why exactly the three rule-150-type rules survive at 61 (because 3 ∈ ⟨4⟩ mod 61), and (iii) predicts confinement at other primes, which I checked where I could. The remaining seven (4850, 5972, 13822, 22064, 25183, 27390, 28586) I could **not** explain; I report what I excluded.

Everything below marked ✔ was computed in this session (code: GF(2^d) arithmetic in GF(2)[x]/Φ_m, eigenvalues at class representatives, cube test λ^{(2^s−1)/3} = 1; bivariate factoring by Kronecker substitution y = x^N into sympy's univariate GF(2) factoriser and subset recombination).

## 1. The mechanism: split cofactor × cube (proved; explains 9 of 16)

**Theorem 1 (✔ exact identities in GF(2)[x^±1,y^±1], hence mod every (x^m−1, y^m−1)).** With
K₁ = 1 + O(1,0), K₂ = 1 + O(1,1), K₃ = O(1,0) + O(1,1), and cofactors C_O = O(1,0), C₉₀ = t(x)t(y), C₁₅₀ = (1+t(x))(1+t(y)) = R(7):

| rule | identity | cofactor type |
|---|---|---|
| 1112 | C_O · K₁³ | rule 90 (diagonals) |
| 10498 | C_O · K₂³ | rule 90 (diagonals) |
| 13584 | C_O · K₃³ | rule 90 (diagonals) |
| 2436 | C₉₀ · K₁³ | rule 90 (axes) |
| 17056 | C₉₀ · K₂³ | rule 90 (axes) |
| 28944 | C₉₀ · K₃³ | rule 90 (axes) |
| 3461 | C₁₅₀ · K₁³ | rule 150 (axes) |
| 26883 | C₁₅₀ · K₂³ | rule 150 (axes) |
| 18246 | C₁₅₀ · K₃³ | rule 150 (axes) |

(Irreducible factorisations over GF(2): e.g. x⁴y⁴·R(1112) = (1+xy)(x+y)·(x+y+xy+xy²+x²y)³; the cores K₁, K₂, K₃ are irreducible.)

**Lemma (trivial).** If f = C·K³ in the group ring, every nonzero eigenvalue of f at m is a cube whenever every nonzero eigenvalue of C is. (λ_f = λ_C · λ_K³, and 0 is excluded.)

**Lemma (cofactors; m odd).** Let h = (m+1)/2, so 2h ≡ 1 (mod m).
- C₉₀ has eigenvalues t(ζ^j) t(ζ^k): cubes iff rule 90 is confined at m.
- C_O = O(1,0) ≡ t(x^h y^h) · t(x^h y^{−h}) (mod x^m−1, y^m−1) ✔(m=37). Its eigenvalues are t(ζ^{h(j+k)}) t(ζ^{h(j−k)}), so O(1,0) is confined at m **iff** rule 90 is.
- C₁₅₀ has eigenvalues (1+t(ζ^j))(1+t(ζ^k)): cubes iff rule 150 is confined at m. Also (1+t(x))·t(x^h) ≡ t(x^{3h}) (mod x^m−1) ✔(m=37: (1+x+x⁻¹)(x¹⁹+x⁻¹⁹) ≡ x²⁰+x⁻²⁰), so wherever rule 90 is confined, so is rule 150 (nonzero ratio of cubes; at j=0 the value is 1).

So the six rule-90-type rules are confined exactly where rule 90 is; the three rule-150-type rules exactly where rule 150 is. The rest is 1-D arithmetic.

## 2. The 1-D arithmetic: cubic character of rule-90/150 eigenvalues (proved)

Let m be an odd prime, d = ord_m(2) **even** (so ω ∈ F₄ ⊂ F = F(2^d)), ζ a primitive m-th root of unity in F, U = ⟨4⟩ ≤ (Z/m)*, |U| = d/2, and χ(α) = α^{(2^d−1)/3}. For α ∈ F(2^s)* with d/s ∈ {1,2} (the case for symmetric rules), α is a cube in F(2^s) iff χ(α) = 1.

**Theorem 2.** For j ≢ 0 (mod m):
 (a) χ(ζ^j + ζ^{−j}) = ( ∏_{u∈U} (1+ζ^{uj}) )²;
 (b) χ(1 + ζ^j + ζ^{−j}) = ∏_{u∈U}(1+ζ^{uj}+ζ^{2uj}) = ∏_{u∈3U}(1+ζ^{uj}) / ∏_{u∈U}(1+ζ^{uj}).

*Proof.* (2^d−1)/3 = 1+4+…+4^{d/2−1}, so χ(α) = ∏_{i<d/2} α^{4^i} = N_{F/F₄}(α). Apply to ζ^j+ζ^{−j} = ζ^{−j}(1+ζ^j)² and 1+ζ^j+ζ^{−j} = ζ^{−j}(1+ζ^j+ζ^{2j}), using χ(ζ^{−j}) = 1 (ζ has order m, 3 ∤ m) and 1+z+z² = (1+z³)/(1+z). ∎
✔ (b) verified numerically for every prime m < 200 with d even.

**Corollary 2.1 (rule 150).** If 3 ∈ ⟨4⟩ (mod m), every nonzero eigenvalue of rule 150 at m is a cube. If 2 is a primitive root mod m, ⟨4⟩ = the squares, so the condition is (3|m) = 1, i.e. (with m ≡ 5 mod 8 forced) **m ≡ 13 (mod 24)**. This holds at 13, 37, 61, 181, 421, 541, 613, 661, 853, 1117 (and at 193, 313 where 2 is not primitive). ✔ checked at 13, 37, 61, 181, 193, 313, 421, 853.

**Corollary 2.2 (rule 90; proved modulo Dirichlet's class-number formula, ✔ verified at all 18 applicable primes < 1000 that I tested).** Let 2 be a primitive root mod m, m ≡ 5 (mod 8), K = Q(√m), ε its fundamental unit, h its class number. Then U = squares, ∏_{u∈U}(1−ζ^u) = ±√m·ε^{∓h} (class-number formula), √m ≡ 1 (mod 2O_K) since m ≡ 5 mod 8, and O_K/2 = F₄ with (−1+√m)/2 ↦ ω. Hence
 rule 90 is confined to cubes at m ⇔ ε^h ≡ 1 (mod 2O_K) ⇔ **3 | h or ε ∈ Z[√m]** (i.e. ε = a+b√m with a,b integers, not a half-integer unit).
 At 37: ε = 6+√37, h = 1 ⇒ confined (this is *why* Π₃₇ = (2¹⁸−1)/3 in Martin–Odlyzko–Wolfram). At 61: ε = (39+5√61)/2 ⇒ not confined ✔. At 101: 10+√101 ✔; 197: 14+√197 ✔; 269: 82+5√269 ✔. At 5, 13, 29, 53, 149, 173, 181: half-integer units ⇒ not confined ✔.
 Moreover if additionally m ≡ 2 (mod 3), then 3U = non-squares and (b) gives χ(1+t) = ε^{±2h} mod 2, so rule 150 is confined ⇔ rule 90 is (✔ 101, 197, 557, 709, 877 both; 461, 941 neither).

**Why 61 for exactly three rules.** 61 ≡ 13 (mod 24) ⇒ rule 150 confined (Cor. 2.1); ε₆₁ half-integral ⇒ rule 90 not (Cor. 2.2). So C₁₅₀·K³ rules survive, C₉₀/C_O·K³ rules do not. ✔ direct 2-D check at 61.

The periods then satisfy: odd part of the period of rule 90 at 37 divides 87381 and at 101 divides (2⁵⁰−1)/3; the odd part of the period of each of the nine rules divides (2^s−1)/3 at every m in item 4 where they are listed as confined.

## 4. The seven unexplained rules — what I excluded (✔ all at m = 37)

4850, 5972, 22064, 25183, 28586 are irreducible over GF(2)[x,y]; 13822 = P₁P₂ (P₁ = O(2,0)+O(2,2)+O(1,1) = R(44), P₂ a 17-term radius-2 factor), 27390 = K₃·P₃ (P₃ radius 3, 32 terms). None of the factors is itself confined. Excluded for all seven, modulo (x³⁷−1, y³⁷−1):
- f ≡ C·K³ with K any of the 32,767 family rules and C any family rule confined at 37 (there are 110), or C any of 1139 products of ≤3 one-dimensional factors t, 1+t along axes/diagonals (and dilations by 2, 4);
- f ≡ C₁·C₂ or C₁·C₂·C₃ with Cᵢ confined family rules;
- f ≡ g(x,y)·g(x^d,y^d) with g any family rule and d = ±2^k, k odd (all 18 values);
- f = g·ḡ with g ∈ GF(4)[x,y] (odd-degree GF(2)-irreducible factors of f(x,x^N) occur with odd multiplicity for N ∈ {10,11,13,17}, which a GF(4)-norm forbids).
- They are not confined at 13, 29, 53, 61, 101, 181, 197.

Is it chance? Seven survivors against ≈0.6 expected has Poisson tail ≈ 4·10⁻⁶; I do not believe chance for all seven, but I found no mechanism, and the data (confined at 37 only, no small factorisation) are at least *consistent* with 37-specific structure like mechanism 5 with a non-family B. Concrete next steps: factor x⁴y⁴f over GF(4) and GF(8) (Kronecker + Cantor–Zassenhaus), and search f ≡ g·g(x^d,y^d) with g of radius ≤ 2 but *not* symmetric (the symmetry of f may swap g and its twist) — my search covered symmetric g only.

## 5. Where I think the project is wrong or missing something

1. The claim that mechanisms 1–6 explain none of the 16 is wrong for nine of them: they are mechanism 1 × mechanism 2, invisible to a search that tests mechanisms singly and never factors. Bivariate factoring over GF(2) is easy without Sage: Kronecker substitution y = x^N (N > 2·deg_x), sympy `factor_list(…, modulus=2)`, recombine subsets.
2. "Rule 90 is confined at 37" is treated as a brute fact; Corollary 2.2 is the reason (integral fundamental unit 6+√37), and it also says *which* other primes behave like 37.
3. "At 61 nothing else in the family is confined": R(7) = (1+t(x))(1+t(y)) is confined at 61 ✔ (presumably discounted as a split rule — but it is exactly the cofactor that matters).
4. The chance model's "free classes" should be computed *after* removing cubed factors and split cofactors; for the nine, the class count is irrelevant. For the seven it stands, and 7 ≫ 0.6 says a mechanism is still missing, not that chance is at work.
5. Theorem 2 is stated for prime m only; I have not proved composite-m versions (the norm argument needs modification when Φ_m is reducible).
