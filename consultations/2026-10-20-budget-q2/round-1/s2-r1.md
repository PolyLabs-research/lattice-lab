## Summary

The missing mechanism is the most elementary one: **nine of the sixteen rules are perfect cubes of radius‑1 rules times a small, separately confined cofactor**, as exact Laurent‑polynomial identities over GF(2). The project never factorised in two variables, so it never saw this. The cofactors are of three types, and their confinement has two different number‑theoretic sources, which is exactly why three rules survive at 61 and six do not. The remaining seven are **not** explained by anything I could find; one of them (4850) is confined to *ninth* powers at 37, which rules out chance for it.

Everything below was machine‑checked (Python, F(2^d) built as GF(2)[x]/(irreducible factor of x^m−1), cube test λ^((2^d−1)/3) = 1, discrete logs via Pohlig–Hellman since 2^36−1 is smooth). I reproduced the project's inputs first: all 16 confined at 37, exactly 3461/18246/26883 at 61, rule 90 confined at 37 and not at 61.

## 1. The factorisations (proved; exact identities in GF(2)[x^±1, y^±1])

Define
- A = 1 + O(1,0) (von Neumann neighbourhood with centre), B = 1 + O(1,1), C = O(1,0) + O(1,1) (Moore ring),
- K1 = t(x)·t(y), K2 = (1+t(x))·(1+t(y)), K3 = t(x)+t(y) = O(1,0).

Then, as Laurent polynomials (hence a fortiori mod (x^m−1, y^m−1) for every m):

| rule | identity | rule | identity | rule | identity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1112 | K3·A³ | 10498 | K3·B³ | 13584 | K3·C³ |
| 2436 | K1·A³ | 17056 | K1·B³ | 28944 | K1·C³ |
| 3461 | K2·A³ | 26883 | K2·B³ | 18246 | K2·C³ |

(Found by Kronecker substitution + univariate GF(2) factoring + recombination; each identity re‑verified by direct multiplication.) The other seven: 4850, 5972, 22064, 25183, 28586 are **irreducible over GF(2)**; 13822 = G1·H1 with G1 = 1+O(1,0)+O(2,0)+O(2,1), H1 = O(1,1)+O(2,0)+O(2,2); 27390 = C·H2 with H2 = O(1,0)+O(2,0)+O(2,1)+O(2,2)+O(3,1)+O(3,3).

**Theorem A.** If f = K·g³ and every nonzero eigenvalue of K at m is a cube, then every nonzero eigenvalue of f at m is a cube. *Proof.* f(ζ^j,ζ^k) = K(ζ^j,ζ^k)·g(ζ^j,ζ^k)³; if nonzero, both factors are nonzero, and a product of cubes is a cube. ∎

So the nine reduce to confinement of K1, K2, K3, which are one‑dimensional objects.

## 2. Confinement of the cofactors — two different reasons

Throughout, m odd, gcd(m,3)=1, η ranges over m‑th roots of unity ≠ 1. Note t(η) = η^(−1)(1+η)², and η is a cube (η = (η^u)³, 3u ≡ 1 mod m). Hence **t(η) is a cube ⟺ 1+η is a cube.**

**Theorem B (K2; the "3 ∈ ⟨4⟩" law).** If 3 ≡ 4^a (mod m) for some a ≥ 0, then every nonzero eigenvalue of rule 150 = 1+t(x), hence of K2, is a cube at m.
*Proof.* 1+t(η) = η^(−1)(1+η+η²) = η^(−1)(1+η³)/(1+η). Since η^m = 1, η³ = η^(4^a), so 1+η³ = (1+η)^(4^a) and (1+η³)/(1+η) = (1+η)^(4^a−1) = ((1+η)^((4^a−1)/3))³. ∎
Checked explicitly: 37: 3 = 4^13, 1+ζ³ = (1+ζ)^(4^13); 61: 3 = 4³ = 64, 1+ζ³ = (1+ζ)^64 in F(2^60); 13: a=2; 181: a=28. For prime m with 2 primitive, ⟨4⟩ = quadratic residues, so the condition is (3|m)=1, i.e. **m ≡ ±1 (mod 12)**. Note Theorem B is independent of rule 90: this is why the three K2‑rules are confined at 61 (and 13, 143, 169, 181, 193) where rule 90 is not.

**Theorem C (K1, K3; the fundamental‑unit law).** K1 = t(x)t(y) and K3 = (x+y)(1+x^(−1)y^(−1)) have eigenvalues t(ζ^j)t(ζ^k) and ζ^j(1+ζ^(k−j))(1+ζ^(−j−k)); both are cube‑confined at m as soon as **every 1+η is a cube**, i.e. iff rule 90 is cube‑confined at m. Conversely at prime m with 2 primitive, the cubic character satisfies c(2j) = −c(j), so c(j) = c(1)·(j|m) (verified for all j at the 11 primes 5…197), and K1, K3 are confined iff c(1)=0.
For p prime, p ≡ 5 (mod 8), 2 primitive mod p: 1+ζ is a cube ⟺ N_{F(2^{p−1})/F(4)}(1+ζ) = ∏_{r∈QR}(1+ζ^r) = 1. By Dirichlet, ∏_{r∈QR}(1−ζ^r) = √p·ε_p^(−h_p) in O_K, K = Q(√p); 2 is inert in K, √p = 2θ−1 ≡ 1 mod 2O_K, so the condition is **ε_p^{h_p} ≡ 1 (mod 2O_K)**, i.e. (h_p odd, F(4)* of order 3) either 3 | h_p or ε_p ∈ Z[√p] (integer rather than half‑integer coordinates). Checked against all 11 such primes < 200 (all h=1): ε_37 = 6+√37, ε_101 = 10+√101, ε_197 = 14+√197 are integral → confined (verified); ε_5, ε_13, ε_29, ε_53, ε_61 = (39+5√61)/2, ε_149, ε_173, ε_181 are half‑integral → not confined (verified). Status: the Dirichlet step is classical; the mod‑2 reduction is my argument; I consider it proved modulo the standard formula, and it is checked at 11 primes.

So: **rule 90's confinement at 37 is not a subfield effect and not chance in the eigenvalue sense; it is the single arithmetic fact that the fundamental unit of Q(√37) lies in Z[√37].** The project's sentence "every proper subfield lies inside the cubes, and so do rule 90's eigenvalues" conflates two unrelated things.

At 61: exactly the K2 three, 3461, 18246, 26883 (Theorem B with 3 = 4³ mod 61); the six K1/K3 rules fail at 61 because ε_61 is half‑integral (and I checked they fail).

## 4. The seven residuals — honest status

Not explained. Facts (checked): all seven have 189 nonzero classes (no zero eigenvalues). **4850 has all 189 eigenvalues ninth powers** at 37 (its eigenvalues generate exactly the 9th‑power subgroup of F(2^18)*); under the chance model that is ≈ 9^(−10), so a mechanism exists that I did not find. 13822 and 27390 are products of two non‑confined symmetric factors (above) whose cubic characters cancel class by class — equivalent to f ≡ g³·k with k confined, with no visible reason for k. Negative searches, all with g ranging over the whole 32,767‑rule family and cofactor in {1, K1, K2, K3}: f ≡ g³·c, f ≡ g·g(x^d,y^d)·c for all d, f ≡ g(x^a,y^a)g(x^b,y^b)·c for all a<b≤18, f ≡ g·g(x^10,y^10)·g(x^26,y^26)·c, all modulo (x^37−1, y^37−1): no hits. Confinement is 37‑specific for all seven; I suspect a factorisation over GF(4)/GF(8) or an F(2^9)/half‑Frobenius structure (6 = 2^9, 6² = −1), which I could not test in the time available.

## 5. Where the project is wrong or missing something

- "Mechanism 2: f = g^k" was tested only for f itself; the real pattern is f = g³·(1‑d cofactor). Two‑variable factorisation over GF(2) must be a first‑line tool; I'd guess some of the 18 rules credited to mechanism 5 are also of this form.
- The chance model treats the 16 as independent; the six K1/K3 rules share one ternary event (ε_37 ∈ Z[√37]), and the three K2 rules are forced. The honest residual is 7 against 0.62 — still significant, and 4850's 9th‑power confinement says so emphatically.
- The claim that rule 90's cubes at 37 come with the subfields is wrong; the criterion is the fundamental unit, and the same criterion predicts the full list of primes.
