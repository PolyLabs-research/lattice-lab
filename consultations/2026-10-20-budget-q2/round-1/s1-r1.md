## Summary

The mechanism for most of the 16 is simply **cubes of non‑symmetric polynomials**, times factors that are rule‑90‑like or Φ₃‑like, which are cube‑confined at 37 for provable reasons. Nine of the 16 factor this way as honest Laurent polynomials over GF(2) (not just mod the ideal); the three that persist at 61 are exactly the ones whose extra factors are Φ₃(x)Φ₃(y), and Φ₃ is cube‑confined at 61 by a telescoping‑norm argument that does not need rule 90. The remaining seven are **not** explained by anything I found, and they behave like 37‑specific accidents at every other modulus I tested — though seven is too many for the project's chance model, so I flag that as unresolved. Everything below was recomputed by machine (numpy group‑ring arithmetic mod (x^m−1, y^m−1), cube test f^((2^s−1)/3) idempotent; I first re‑verified all 16 at 37 and the three at 61).

## 1. The cube criterion (proved)

**Lemma A.** Let K = F(2^d) with d even, so F₄ ⊂ K. For λ ∈ K*, λ is a cube in K iff N_{K/F₄}(λ) = ∏_{i<d/2} λ^{4^i} = 1.
*Proof.* N: K* → F₄* is a surjective homomorphism onto a group of order 3, so ker N has index 3; cubes are a subgroup of index 3 (K* cyclic, 3 | |K*|) and N(μ³) = N(μ)³ = 1, so cubes ⊂ ker N, hence equal. ∎

For symmetric rules, "cube in F(2^s)" and "cube in F(2^d)" agree when s is even (d = 2s, (2^{2s}−1)/(2^s−1) = 2^s+1 ≢ 0 mod 3); this covers 37, 61, 13, 101, etc. For a character χ = (ζ^j, ζ^k), N(f(χ)) = ∏_{v∈H} f(χ^v), where H = ⟨4⟩ ⊂ (Z/m)* and χ^v = (ζ^{vj}, ζ^{vk}). So:

**Criterion.** f is cube‑confined at m (d even) iff ∏_{v∈H} f(x^v, y^v) ≡ e_f mod (x^m−1, y^m−1), e_f the idempotent of the support of f. For m = 37, H = quadratic residues (order 18); for 61, order 30.

**Lemma B (telescoping).** If A·f ≡ A(x^c, y^c) mod the ideal with c ∈ H, and A(χ^v) ≠ 0 for all v ∈ H whenever f(χ) ≠ 0, then every nonzero eigenvalue of f at m is a cube. *Proof.* N(f(χ)) = ∏_v A(χ^{cv})/A(χ^v) = 1 because v ↦ cv permutes H. ∎

**Building blocks, all proved/verified:**
- (B1) Monomials x^a y^b: eigenvalue a 37th (61st) root of unity, a cube since 3 ∤ m.
- (B2) Φ₃(w) = w² + w + 1 with w a monomial: (w+1)Φ₃(w) = w³+1, so Lemma B with A = w+1, c = 3 applies whenever **3 ∈ ⟨4⟩ mod m and d is even**. 37 ≡ 61 ≡ 1 (mod 12), and I verified 3 ∈ ⟨4⟩ for both. (Zero of A occurs only at w(χ) = 1, where Φ₃ = 1, a cube.) Independently verified: x²+x+1 is cube‑confined in F(2^36) at 37 and F(2^60) at 61, and t(x)+1 = x^{−1}Φ₃(x) is cube‑confined at 61 **even though t(x) is not**.
- (B3) w + 1 with w a monomial (covers x+1, y+1, xy+1, and x+y = x(x^{−1}y+1)): cube‑confined at 37 iff rule 90 is, since t(w) = w^{−1}(w+1)². The clean certificate is the group‑ring identity ∏_{v∈QR(37)} (x^v+1) ≡ Σ_{i=1}^{36} x^i mod (x^37−1), i.e. N(ζ^j+1) = 1 for j ≠ 0 (verified; it also holds at 101, 197, 269, 349, 373, 389; fails at 13, 61, 109, 181, 193 — consistent with rule 90 being unconfined at 61).

## 5. Where the project is wrong or missing something

1. Mechanism 2 only searched g inside the symmetric family; cube roots need not be D4‑symmetric. Nine of the sixteen are literal cubes of x↔y‑symmetric quintics/octics, visible by ordinary bivariate factorisation — the one thing "nobody has looked" at.
2. "Rule‑90 factors" should include x+1, xy+1, x+y: w+1 = w^{1/2}·t(w^{1/2}) in the group ring (square roots exist since m is odd).
3. The right invariant is the norm to F₄, not subfield membership: it gives a one‑line certificate for rule 90 at 37 and explains 61 without rule 90.
4. The residual seven exceed the chance model; it should be audited before the chance explanation is accepted.
