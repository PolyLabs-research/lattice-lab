A claim counts here only when something outside the work has checked it. One of those checks is running right here.

How we work

Three rules, in every programme

Finding a pattern, a gain or a plausible answer is easy. Knowing whether it is real is hard: with enough rules, seeds and settings to try, chance supplies patterns too, and a system that reports on itself will report success.

  1. 01

    Nobody marks their own work.

    Each programme hands its output to a check the work cannot change: a proof checker, a rule checker, games against fixed opponents, a quote found on its page.

  2. 02

    Written down before the run.

    Predictions, thresholds and what each outcome will decide are committed first. A gate that turns out to measure the wrong thing is retired with its reasons; it is never moved to let something through.

  3. 03

    Misses stay on the page.

    A refuted prediction is reported as plainly as a confirmed one. A correction is dated and added beside what it corrects, never made silently.

Much of the code in these programmes is written by AI coding agents working from written plans, and in lattice-lab agents propose mechanisms and prove them. The same rules apply to them: their work counts because the checks pass, not because they say it does.
