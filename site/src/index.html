PolyLabs Research

Small systems, judged by checks they cannot change

We ask what minimal systems can actually do: the laws of a cellular automaton, a chess network of 6.5 million parameters, a model that learns algebra by search, one evolved cell, a company run by AI agents, an agent that reads a mountain of documents. In every programme a claim counts only when something outside the work has checked it: a proof checker, a rule checker, games against fixed opponents, a quote found on its page, or a prediction written down before the run.

Mathematics · cellular automata · quantum codes

lattice-lab

proved in Lean, in part

Exact laws of cellular automata on finite lattices, found by experiment, tested by registered prediction and proved in Lean, with AI agents proposing and proving. The same ring holds bivariate bicycle quantum codes, and a census of every weight-6 code up to 400 qubits found [[294,10,20]], above the published [[288,12,18]] on k d²/n.

Judged by Lean 4, and a hash-chained ledger of predictions Self-play · search · chess

Tiny AlphaZero

on a plateau

A 6.5-million-parameter network trained by self-play on one small computer, one registered change per round for 25 rounds. A Stockfish teacher helped and twice the teacher hurt. On a public scale it performs between 1,154 and 1,780 by opponent, so the record gives no single number yet.

Judged by games against frozen opponents, with intervals Search · symbolic mathematics

Reckoner

gain real, cause open

A five-million-parameter model learning algebra by search, one checked rewrite at a time, so its reasoning can be read. Its first campaign beat par more often, 101 to 209 of 600; the gain turned out to be shorter paths, and an erratum found it had trained without its intended target.

Judged by a checker for every answer, and par for every problem Artificial life · neural networks

Protocell

beat the best simple rule, once

One simulated cell whose wall and brain are one network, evolved to manage light, to test whether behaviour emerges from parts working together. After evolution from nothing failed, a cell written weight by weight, then searched, beat the best simple rule in 2 of 3 runs, from a designed start.

Judged by worlds that pass their own checks first, and fresh starts Multi-agent AI · governance

the_organisation

open source, one live run

An open-source company of AI agents: leads who gate each phase, a board for disputes, and a person who pays and signs. It is built to study how agents behave under governance, and its one run on a real model so far ended with the work sent back twice and the release conditional.

Judged by evidence agents cannot verify themselves, and gates that run the check Document investigation · AI agents

Corpus Lens

prototype; no model benchmarked

Search and exploration for large collections of documents, scanned or not, with an investigation agent that may record a finding only with a quote the server finds on the page. The benchmark a model must pass first is fixed in advance and has not yet been run.

How we work

Nobody marks their own work

Finding a pattern, a gain or a plausible answer is easy. Knowing whether it is real is hard: with enough rules, seeds and settings to try, chance supplies patterns too, and a system that reports on itself will report success. So each programme hands its output to a check that the work cannot change, and writes down what it expects before it looks.

Three habits that follow

  1. Misses are reported as plainly as results. Each programme page lists its misses beside its results: the refuted predictions, failed gates and errata, and what each one pointed at.
  2. A gate is never moved to let something through. A gate that turns out to measure the wrong thing is retired with its reasons written down, and its threshold stays where it was.
  3. Corrections are added, not made silently. An erratum is dated and appended, and a finding that turns out wrong is withdrawn where it stands.

Much of the code in these programmes is written by AI coding agents working from written plans, and in lattice-lab agents propose mechanisms and prove them. The same rule applies to them: their work counts because the checks pass, not because they say it does.
