Programme · Tiny AlphaZero

How strong can a 6.5-million-parameter chess network get on one small computer?

Tiny AlphaZero rebuilds the AlphaZero loop (a network that plays itself, guided by tree search, and learns from the games) at the smallest size that is still complete, on one mini-PC. Its first version could not tell whether it was improving. This one is built around that question: a fixed ladder of frozen opponents, intervals on every score, and one change per round, registered before it runs.

How improvement is measured

The first version decided which network was best by four-game matches, so its "best model" changed by coin flip. Here progress is read against a fixed ladder: frozen opponents, from earlier networks to Stockfish 16 held to a set skill level, playing 100 four-move openings, each twice with colours swapped. Every score comes with a 95% interval from a paired bootstrap over the openings, and a round's verdict compares two networks opening by opening.

Each round changes one thing. Its file is committed before launch, with the hypothesis, the primary test, forecasts and a table of what each outcome would decide. A gate is never moved to let a network through: when two early gates proved to measure the wrong thing, they were retired to reported-only with the argument written down, and their thresholds were left where they were.

The network starts from a supervised warm start, and is trained by a cycle of 512 self-play games, searched at 48 simulations a move, then 400 training steps. Every measurement runs on the mini-PC, which has an integrated AMD GPU; from late October the self-play training ran on rented GPUs. It is not pure self-play: mate puzzles are rehearsed from the second round, and from round p2_t a quarter of every batch is positions labelled by Stockfish. Since p2_aa the network has been trained on Stockfish's labels alone, with no self-play games: millions of positions its earlier self-play reached, each seen a few times. That training runs on the mini-PC; only the labelling is rented.

The numbers in this chart

Each round's two arms, against the same opponent on the same machine, 200 games a pass of the openings. To p2_w each arm stands on six passes; in p2_aa each stands on twelve, and in p2_ab the change's twelve are set against 24 from the two runs of the four-million-position training, taken from the record rather than measured again. Neither of the last two rounds played self-play games. Of seven changes, three helped (the Stockfish teacher in a quarter of each batch, four million labelled positions alone, then eight million), one hurt (twice the teacher), and three made no difference the test could separate. p2_ab's interval clears zero by 0.0011.

Where it stands on a public scale: a spread, not a number

1,400 games, registered

To place the network on CCRL's rating list, the network as it stood after p2_w played seven opponents of published rating, 200 games each. The registered test asked whether the classical engines agree among themselves about its rating. None of the three agrees with the other two, so the decision table's answer is that there is no single number at this level, and the page publishes each opponent's reading instead.

Where it stands

The plateau after p2_t has ended, on one route. Trained on Stockfish-labelled positions alone, four million positions each seen about four times beat the start at skill 5 in p2_aa, a second run of a gain the capacity-or-data diagnostic had found in an aside; it was the same positions in a new order, so the data were not replicated. In p2_ab eight million positions beat that training by +0.0232, with the interval's lower end at +0.0011, on one run. Against the start the gain is +0.0635, the largest the project has measured, reported rather than registered.

Why many positions each seen a few times help, when a small set drilled many times hurt, is a hypothesis. The diagnostic's own question, size or data, is not resolved: its larger sets were stopped by the sample cap while still improving. The next round, p2_ac, is more data again, its size not yet settled. On the benchmark track the clock-scaled rerun is registered and not scheduled, and the network that now stands has not been placed on the CCRL scale. Reckoner carries the same search, and the same rules for registering a round, over to algebra.
