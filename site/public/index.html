PolyLabs Research

Finding the laws of simple worlds, and proving them

We study cellular automata on finite lattices: square, hexagonal and triangular. Each claim goes through a loop of machine checks: a simulation, a test registered in advance, and a proof in Lean. More and more of the proposing and proving is done by AI agents, and machines judge their work too.

The model

A loop in which nobody marks their own work

Finding a pattern in data is easy. Knowing whether it is real is hard: with enough rules, sizes and laws to try, chance supplies patterns too. So every stage of our loop hands its output to a check that the stage cannot change:

  1. Experiment. LatticeLife runs the automata and says what each number means, including when it is only a bound.
  2. Law. Kepler fits closed-form laws with error bars, and ranks them against their rivals.
  3. Registered test. A law's predictions at untested sizes are written to a hash-chained ledger and pushed to GitHub before anyone computes those sizes.
  4. Proof. What survives is proved in Lean 4. A theorem counts only if it builds and rests on Lean's three standard axioms.
  5. Agents. AI agents, given a problem card and the proof library, propose the mechanism and write the proof. An automatic judge decides whether it counts.

The model, in detail, includes the real ledger of one study, to check yourself, and a record of every step an agent took.

Findings

What the loop has found

proved, in part

The parity laws

Exact period laws for the parity rule on all three lattices. The hexagon's first law was refuted, and the corrected one is proved for every even n. The square rule escapes the shortfalls of rule 90 and the hexagon shares them exactly; a brief literature search found neither fact.

 three rounds, refined twice

The linear atlas

Periods of 502 linear rules built from neighbour shells. A rule reaches its field's full bound unless something confines its eigenvalues. Four mechanisms do, and three of them were found because a registered prediction failed.

 replicated

Settling time

A random soup under B2/S34 settles in a time that grows like the logarithm of the torus size, replicated to L = 1024. Across eight random rules on four lattices the fluctuations share one shape and one ratio, the Gumbel values of extreme-value theory.

 7 of 7 accepted, one in round 2

Agents that prove

Seven fresh agents, each given only a problem card and the Lean library, proved theorems the library lacked. Six passed the judge in their first round. The seventh was stopped for cost with its Lean unbuilt, and made it build in a second round. The third found its law from data first. The fourth answered a question nobody had answered; the fifth answered part of a harder one; the sixth answered the question the fifth's test raised; the seventh explained four of five ninth-power rules.

 answered by agents

Cubes and ninth powers at m = 37

Six rules that none of the known mechanisms touches had every eigenvalue a cube at m = 37, far beyond chance. In the fourth pilot an agent found why and proved it in Lean; its condition made 633 claims, none false. Five more there, rule 4850 and four rules of radius 5, have every nonzero eigenvalue a ninth power, which no known mechanism explained. The seventh pilot's agent explained the four, and proved them in Lean. A multi-agent deep dive explained the fifth, rule 4850, with a proof that three independent checkers found stands, with repairs. That proof is not yet in Lean.

 Lean 4, standard axioms

The proof library

The bridge from periods to eigenvalues, the bound, the reduction from even sizes to odd, every confinement mechanism but rule 4850's, and the agents' seven proofs, all checked by Lean against mathlib.

 k and d reproduced

Quantum codes

Every linear rule here lives in one ring, and it is the ring of bivariate bicycle quantum codes, IBM's gross code among them. A code's logical qubits are read off the same characters. A registered test agreed with plain linear algebra on 3,000 of 3,000 random codes and reproduced all 12 published values, and a second reproduced all 12 published distances, by a search written here.

Every number on this site comes from the project's registered records or is computed in your browser from the rules themselves. Experiments run on LatticeLife; laws are fitted by Kepler; both are open source. The studies, ledgers and Lean library are in lattice-lab, which is private for now.

The agents in the pilots are Claude Code sessions, each started fresh with no context but a problem card; the seventh pilot's second round ran over the API. Their proofs count because Lean accepts them, not because they say so.
